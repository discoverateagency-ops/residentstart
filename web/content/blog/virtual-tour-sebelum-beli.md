Buying a house that is still under construction means deciding on something you cannot fully see yet. Residentstart's demo tries to close part of that gap with a 360° viewer built from photos of a real show unit. This article explains what the tour is, where its limits are, and how to get the most out of it.

## Why brochures mislead

A brochure is designed to sell. It is not usually dishonest, but it is selective. Common problems include:

- wide-angle lenses that make rooms look larger than they feel,
- rendered images with furniture scaled to fit,
- photos that never show the ceiling height, the stairs or the service area,
- no sense of how one room connects to the next.

The last point matters more than people expect. A floor plan tells you the area of each room. It does not tell you what you see when you open the front door, or how far the kitchen is from the carport with a bag of groceries.

## What a 360° tour shows

Our tours are built from normal photos of the show unit, displayed as partial panoramas inside a 360° viewer. The [Cilandak Residence tour](/buy/cilandak-residence/tour) has 13 stops that walk through the real show unit: 3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, a 90 m² building on 72 m² of land (6 × 12 m), two floors and a carport.

Inside the viewer you can:

- **drag** to look around each stop,
- tap the **green arrows** to walk from room to room,
- jump to any stop using the **thumbnail strip**,
- see where you are on the **floor-plan minimap**.

Because the stops follow the order you would walk in real life, you get a sense of sequence: entrance, living area, kitchen, stairs, bedrooms. That is the part a brochure rarely gives you.

> A tour does not replace a site visit. It helps you arrive at the site visit with better questions.

A note on the format: the current stops are partial panoramas, not full spheres. A full 360° photo can replace any stop later without changing how the viewer works.

## Show-unit vs built unit

It is important to know what you are looking at. The demo uses three labels:

1. **Show unit.** On Cilandak Residence, the photos come from the real show unit of that project.
2. **Show-unit reference.** Other homes of the same unit type, such as [Cipete Garden Home](/buy), use the same tour as a reference for layout and finish. Cipete is in construction and is a larger type (4 bedrooms, 3 bathrooms, land 150 m², building 180 m²), so treat the tour as a guide to style, not to exact dimensions.
3. **Show-unit preview, design reference.** Projects that are still open for funding, like [Bintaro Green Cluster](/invest/bintaro-green-cluster), show the tour as a design reference only. Nothing has been built yet.

A show unit is often furnished and finished with care. The unit you receive may differ in furniture, fittings, orientation and view. Always check the written specification.

## How to use it on a phone

Most people open the tour on a phone, so it is designed for that:

1. Open the home page from [/buy](/buy) and tap the tour button.
2. Turn the phone to landscape for a wider view.
3. Drag with one finger to look around. Look up and down as well as left and right.
4. Tap a green arrow to move to the next room.
5. Open the minimap when you lose track of where you are.
6. Use the thumbnail strip to return to a room you want to see again.

Take screenshots of anything you want to ask about. It is much easier to discuss "this corner of the kitchen" with a picture than from memory.

## Questions to ask the agent after the tour

Once you have walked through, write down your questions before you talk to anyone. Some useful ones:

- Which items in the show unit are included in the price, and which are decoration only?
- Is my unit the same orientation as the show unit, or mirrored?
- What are the ceiling heights on each floor?
- Where do the water tank, electrical panel and drainage go?
- What changes between the show unit and the final specification?
- For homes in construction, what stage is the build at today?
- What does the SHM certificate cover, and when is it transferred?
