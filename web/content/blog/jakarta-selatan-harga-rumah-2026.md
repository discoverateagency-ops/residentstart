South Jakarta is one of the most discussed housing areas in the country. This article looks at the national shortage that frames the conversation, explains why Residentstart's demo listings are located where they are, and describes what changes when a developer sells directly to buyers. It does not forecast prices.

## The 12.7 M national backlog (REI, 2023)

Indonesia's housing backlog, the gap between households that need a home and homes available to them, is estimated at about 12.7 million units, according to REI (Real Estat Indonesia) in 2023.

A backlog of that size is not only a supply problem. It is also a financing problem. Many households that could afford a monthly payment cannot get through a bank's approval process, or cannot save the full price in cash. That is the gap Residentstart's demo is exploring: a different way to finance the part of the price that buyers pay over time. The buyer side is explained in [How to buy a new house in Jakarta without a bank loan](/blog/cara-beli-rumah-tanpa-bank).

## Why Cilandak, Jagakarsa, Cipete

The three South Jakarta listings in the demo sit in Cilandak, Jagakarsa and Cipete. These are established residential districts rather than new frontier areas. They share a few characteristics that make them a reasonable setting for small clusters and townhouses:

- mostly landed housing, with plots that suit two-floor homes,
- existing neighbourhoods with schools, markets and clinics already in place,
- a mix of older houses and smaller new developments on infill land,
- demand from families who already live or work in the south of the city.

Alongside them, the demo includes two projects on the edge of the metro area that are still open for funding: Bintaro Green Cluster in Pondok Aren, Tangerang Selatan, and Depok Hills Cluster in Cimanggis, Depok. You can see those on [/invest](/invest).

## What "developer-direct" changes

In a typical purchase, several parties sit between the developer and the buyer: a sales agent, a bank, sometimes a broker for the mortgage. Each adds steps and paperwork. In the Residentstart model, the developer sells directly and the installment is financed by a DAO pool rather than a bank.

> Developer-direct does not make a house cheaper by itself. It changes who you deal with and how many steps sit between you and the keys.

In practice, that means:

1. **One counterparty.** The buyer deals with the developer for both the house and the payment plan.
2. **Visible funding.** Investors vote on whether a project is funded, and the result is recorded on NEAR testnet.
3. **Visible repayments.** Each paid month creates an on-chain record with a receipt hash, without personal data.
4. **Fixed, published terms.** The same down payment, demo rate and tenor options apply to every home.

There are trade-offs. A bank offers established consumer protections, dispute processes and regulation. A production version of this model would need OJK securities-crowdfunding licensing and a lawyer's review before taking real funds.

## Outlook

We do not predict where prices in South Jakarta will go, and nothing in this article should be read as a forecast. What can be said with more confidence is that the questions shaping the market are structural:

- whether financing can reach households that banks do not currently serve,
- how much infill land remains for small clusters in established districts,
- how quickly projects on the metro edge, such as Pondok Aren and Cimanggis, are completed and connected.

For buyers, the practical step is the same in any market: compare the full cost over time, not only the monthly figure. Our article [5, 10 or 15 years: choosing a tenor](/blog/cicilan-5-10-15-tahun) walks through that comparison.
